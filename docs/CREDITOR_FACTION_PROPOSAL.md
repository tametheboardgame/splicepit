# Creditor Faction Proposal — The Clearing House

**Status: PROVISIONAL — requires approval before becoming canon.**

## 1. Core concept

The creditor faction should not feel like a generic leather-jacketed post-apocalyptic gang. SplicePit's world already treats disturbing things as routine; the more distinctive threat is an institution that looks almost boring.

**The Clearing House** is a post-collapse network that records, buys, sells and enforces debt between settlements, traders, Fit Pits, hauliers and businesses.

There is little meaningful central government, but commerce still needs trust. The Clearing House became powerful because its records are recognised across otherwise fragmented communities.

If the House says you owe somebody, most merchants and Pit operators treat that debt as real.

If the House says an obligation has been cleared, it is cleared.

If the House says you have defaulted, your options become much narrower.

The faction is effectively a bank, arbitration service, bookmaker, employment broker and organised-crime syndicate that insists it is none of those things.

---

## 2. Why it fits SplicePit

The faction supports the game's blackly comic institutional tone:

- frightening people carrying immaculate ledgers and stamped forms
- contractual language describing deeply coercive arrangements
- receipts for threats, confiscations and forced fight assignments
- polite clerks who may be more alarming than obvious enforcers
- violence presented as the final stage of an administrative process

They should not constantly shout or posture. They are dangerous because they are organised in a world where almost nobody else is.

A House representative might say something like:

> “Default is not a crime. It merely changes which choices remain yours.”

This is tone reference, not locked dialogue.

---

## 3. Why the SpliceMaster owed them

**OPEN detail.** Several explanations remain possible and can later connect to the wider story:

- financed laboratory equipment or pit repairs
- purchased rare animals/genetic stock on credit
- borrowed to cover Fit Pit stakes/losses
- financed an expansion that failed
- inherited/refinanced an older obligation
- borrowed for something suspicious that becomes plot-relevant later

The exact origin should not be locked until the wider story is developed.

What matters for Act 1 is that the debt is legally/locally regarded as attached to the inherited operation rather than disappearing with the SpliceMaster's death.

---

## 4. Why they do not simply seize the pit

A damaged laboratory without a competent operator is worth less than a working SplicePit producing purses, animals, samples and influence.

The player is therefore more valuable alive, operational and indebted than dispossessed.

This creates a credible reason for the House to allow Act 1's deadline rather than simply taking everything during the opening.

---

## 5. Act 1 relationship

Soon after the player inherits the pit, a House representative establishes:

- the amount currently outstanding
- the deadline
- what assets/rights secure the obligation
- what happens on default

The House does not need to be the primary villain of Act 1. The debt is pressure while the player learns the world, earns money and restores enough of the pit to function.

Partial payments may be possible, but the exact economy/deadline remains OPEN.

---

## 6. Paid-debt branch

If the debt is cleared before the deadline:

- the House formally releases its claim
- the player retains the pit and their fight earnings
- Act 2 opens into greater geographic and career freedom
- the player can choose Land/Water/Air specialisation or remain mixed
- the House remains part of the world as a powerful commercial organisation rather than vanishing as a defeated villain

Paying them should produce a satisfying formal moment: a ledger entry crossed/stamped clear, lien document returned, etc.

The player has beaten the first major pressure through competence rather than destroyed the faction.

---

## 7. Unpaid-debt branch

Default is **not game-over**.

The House exercises contractual rights over the pit/player's work. Exact legal fiction can be tuned, but the gameplay result is that the player becomes an assigned House operator/fighter until the obligation is worked down.

Possible consequences:

- House selects or strongly constrains available fights
- a portion of purses/rewards is automatically applied against debt
- certain jobs involve transporting, testing or producing creatures/material for House interests
- access to some independent circuits/markets is restricted
- the player may be sent into more dangerous or morally questionable bouts sooner than an independent player would choose

However, the branch also provides things the independent route may not:

- access to House-controlled pits
- rare samples held as collateral/rewards
- transport into regions the player might otherwise reach later
- specialist contacts
- exposure to the internal politics of the faction
- alternative quests/story information

This keeps the unpaid route from being a simple “bad ending that continues”. It is a different Act 2 with less autonomy but unique opportunity/content.

---

## 8. Internal structure

Exact titles are provisional, but the organisation could include:

### Clerks

Maintain records, register obligations, process payments and issue notices. Often ordinary-looking and unnervingly calm.

### Factors

Negotiate loans/contracts, assess assets and restructure debts. They understand that a functioning SplicePit may be more valuable than seized equipment.

### Match Brokers

Connect debtors, Pit operators, gamblers and House interests. They become especially relevant in the unpaid Act 2 route.

### Collectors

Handle physical repossession and enforcement when paperwork stops working. They need not all be simple thugs; some may be skilled handlers/fighters with dangerous splice creatures.

### Senior House / Clearing Council

The higher structure can remain intentionally obscure until the story needs it. The organisation should initially feel larger than the player's local problem.

---

## 9. Moral character

The Clearing House should not be uniformly evil.

Some employees genuinely believe that enforceable credit is one of the few things keeping trade between settlements functioning.

They are not entirely wrong.

Without them:

- long-distance merchants may not extend credit
- Pit purses/stakes become harder to guarantee
- large animal/material trades become riskier
- debts between settlements can turn directly into violence

The problem is that the same system gives the House enormous coercive power with almost no democratic/legal oversight.

This makes individual House characters capable of being professional, sympathetic, ruthless, corrupt, cruel or surprisingly fair without changing what the institution is.

---

## 10. Visual/presentation identity

The House should visually contrast with improvised pastoral biotech.

Potential motifs:

- portable ledger cases
- stamps/seals
- numbered claim tags
- unusually clean paper
- grey/black practical clothing rather than theatrical gang costumes
- brass/steel tokens or account markers
- standardised cages/transport labels
- precise signage in House-controlled pits

Their spaces should feel ordered rather than luxurious.

The joke is partly that the apocalypse destroyed central government but apparently failed to kill accounts receivable.

---

## 11. Mechanical hooks

The faction can naturally connect to:

- debt clock UI
- purse deductions
- reputation/access
- House-owned Fit Pits
- trade credit
- rare-material auctions
- repossession threats
- branch-exclusive jobs
- creditor-controlled transport routes
- black/grey market sample movement

None of these should be implemented merely because they are listed. They are hooks available when the story/economy needs them.

---

## 12. Decisions required before canonisation

If the core faction is approved, later design still needs to settle:

1. Final faction name (`The Clearing House` is proposed).
2. What the original debt financed.
3. Exact security/collateral attached to the debt.
4. Local representative(s).
5. Debt amount/deadline.
6. Exact default terms.
7. How much of the organisation the player encounters during Act 1 versus later acts.

## Recommendation

Approve **The Clearing House** as the creditor faction concept while leaving the original reason for the SpliceMaster's borrowing open for later story development.
