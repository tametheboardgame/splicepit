# Act 1 Story Framework — PLANNED / DETAILS PROVISIONAL

This expands only the story structure explicitly authorised for Act 1. Names, exact dialogue, creditor identity, debt amount and deadline remain open.

## Act 1 purpose

Act 1 must:

- establish the accident and player's inheritance
- force the first irreversible splice under pressure
- teach that ordinary animals + uncertain source material can create unique outcomes
- introduce local Fit Pits as semi-legal institutions
- reveal the inherited debt
- make test-animal experimentation and genetic-material acquisition part of normal play
- give multiple ways to earn money/progress
- end in a meaningful branch based on whether debt is paid on time

The debt clock creates pressure but should not encourage the player to rush past the actual game. The deadline must allow exploration and experimentation.

---

# Prologue — The Gas

### Beat 1: apprenticeship routine

Very short playable/text introduction establishing:

- player is one of several apprentices
- SpliceMaster is preparing for an upcoming local Fit Pit commitment
- lab/pit is operational but financially strained
- multiple animals/splices are present

Do not front-load lore.

### Beat 2: containment failure

A SpliceMaster creature goes out of control and kills the SpliceMaster.

The situation escalates beyond the apprentices' ability to contain it.

### Beat 3: emergency fail-safe

Player triggers/releases the lab's gas fail-safe.

When the player wakes/returns:

- rampaging splice is dead
- other apprentices are dead
- SpliceMaster is dead
- much of the lab is damaged/contaminated
- player alone survived

This creates immediate guilt/suspicion even if using the fail-safe was the correct action.

---

# Chapter 1 — The Match That Didn't Die With Him

The SpliceMaster's imminent local Land Fit Pit commitment still exists.

Reasons the player cannot simply ignore it can combine:

- money already advanced/wagered
- cancellation creates immediate liability
- promoter/creditor expects the pit to honour the booking
- the pit needs the purse/reputation immediately

Exact mechanism remains open, but it should feel like inherited practical pressure rather than a magical tutorial quest.

## Emergency starter

Player discovers surviving ordinary caged animals and the Master's stored gene/source records/material.

Current proposal: choose one of Rabbit / Goat / Pig and one of ten source packages.

The attempt is irreversible and variable, creating the player's first individually persistent main creature.

## First fight

A local early-tier Land pit is relatively controlled.

Purpose:

- teach capability-driven turn-based combat
- demonstrate that the creature's actual anatomy/instincts determine actions
- establish the Fit Pit as socially normal enough to exist but ethically dubious
- produce some money, not enough to solve the inherited problem

---

# Chapter 2 — The Ledger

After the immediate crisis, the player gets access to the SpliceMaster's accounts/records or receives a visit from the people owed money.

Revelation:

- the pit carries a genuine outstanding debt/obligation
- there is a concrete deadline
- creditors have enough local power to enforce consequences even without strong central government

Player is not told “pay or game over”. The threat is loss of autonomy/control.

The debt clock begins visibly and should be understandable at all times.

---

# Chapter 3 — Learn Before You Ruin It

The game now deliberately teaches the testing loop.

Likely sequence:

1. obtain cheap/common test animal(s)
2. obtain/harvest a common source material
3. perform repeated experiments
4. compare outcomes
5. see the lab record become more informative
6. realise that testing improves confidence but never certainty

The player can still gamble directly on their main creature, but the consequences are explicit.

This is where the game proves it is not a deterministic crafting system.

---

# Chapter 4 — Four Ways to Get What You Need

Introduce the four acquisition channels through natural quests/needs:

- **Buy** something common at a market
- **Harvest** something locally
- **Win** material/sample access through a Pit
- **Trade** for something that cannot simply be purchased

The player does not need a tutorial banner saying “four acquisition methods”; the structure should make them discover the economy naturally.

---

# Chapter 5 — Rebuild Enough to Function

Early upgrades are introduced through necessity rather than a generic tech tree screen.

Examples:

- utilities/containment repair
- basic sample refrigeration
- test-animal cages
- basic diagnostics/records
- treatment pen

The player begins choosing what kind of pit/lab operator they are becoming.

---

# Chapter 6 — The Local Circuit

Open Bramblewick + Latchmarket + nearby routes and one or more branch regions.

Player can earn money through:

- local Land bouts
- quests
- material harvesting/trading
- specialist jobs
- selling/trading surplus where appropriate

The best route should depend partly on what creature/results the player has created.

Act 1 should contain optional content; debt pressure should not force one exact optimisation route.

---

# Chapter 7 — Reputation / Suspicion

The opening disaster continues to matter.

Some NPCs:

- believe the player's account
- think they panicked/killed everyone
- suspect deliberate wrongdoing
- simply care that the pit is open and useful

Do not resolve the mystery/suspicion immediately. It creates social texture and hooks for later authored story.

---

# Act 1 climax — Debt Deadline

The deadline arrives after enough in-game time/opportunity that the outcome reflects player priorities and decisions.

## Branch A — Paid

Player pays the required amount.

Consequences:

- immediate creditor leverage ends
- player retains independent control of the pit
- Act 2 opens broadly
- player can choose Land / Water / Air circuit emphasis or research/trade direction
- payment should feel like an achievement, not the end of economic gameplay

## Branch B — Unpaid

Player cannot/will not pay.

Consequences:

- no game over
- creditor takes practical control/leverage over player/pit
- player must take designated fights/jobs to work down obligation
- some order/choices are constrained
- creditor route opens unique fights, characters, material and story unavailable to the free branch

Important rule: **failure should create different content, not simply less content.**
